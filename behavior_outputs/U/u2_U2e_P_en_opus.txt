SQLite is enough by default. With 30 users, attendance writes are short and rare, and one server makes it a good fit. Turn on WAL mode and busy_timeout. Choose PostgreSQL IF any of these apply: more than one app server or process writes at the same time; another host or BI tool needs network access to the DB; you need to grow a lot (thousands of users, heavy reporting); you need row-level locking, roles and permissions, replication, or point-in-time recovery; your ops team already runs Postgres. What decides it: how many processes write at once, whether anything outside the server connects to the DB, your backup and recovery needs, how much you expect to grow, and who will maintain it. With SQLite, schedule backups using the backup API or litestream. Stick to portable SQL through an ORM so you can move to Postgres later.
