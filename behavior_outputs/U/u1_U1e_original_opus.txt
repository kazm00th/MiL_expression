Post errs:

1. Overgeneralization: 1 study → "no longer needed" / "totally obsolete". Need: replication, n tasks/benchmarks, variance, baseline. "2× score" is relative to which baseline? It may not hold outside the tested benchmark.

2. Self-contradiction: raw logs + summarize@use-time is still summarization, just deferred and query-conditioned. So the evidence ≠ "summaries unneeded". It suggests ¬premature/query-agnostic compression, not ¬summaries.

3. Non sequitur: "No RL needed". RL (weight updates) ⊥ memory/context methods; the two are complementary. A gain from memory does not show that weight changes are useless.

4. Straw man: prior continual-learning work ≠ just "plateau → change weights". Also, "methods can still improve" ¬contradicts "current methods plateau". Both can be true.

5. False dichotomy: raw logs vs. SKILL/docs. A hybrid is possible: keep raw logs and distill them into skills.

6. Omitted costs of raw logs + use-time summarization:
   - context/token cost per query
   - latency
   - retrieval quality degrades as logs grow (scaling)
   - storage
   - privacy/security; persistent prompt injection
   - noise/contradictory entries

7. Omitted value of SKILL/docs:
   - compression ≠ just information loss: abstraction, denoising, generalization
   - human-readable, auditable, editable, verifiable
   - shareable across agents/models/teams
   - cheap per-use cost

8. Source missing: no citation or setup given, so the claims can't be checked. I haven't seen the study (¬infer its details). Q: which paper?
