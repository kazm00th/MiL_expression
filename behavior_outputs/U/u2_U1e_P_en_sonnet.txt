Several problems with the post's reasoning:

1. Overgeneralizes from one study. "A study says X" doesn't establish X as settled fact ("no RL needed," "totally obsolete"). One result (likely on specific benchmarks/agent tasks) doesn't refute a whole research direction.

2. False dichotomy: raw-log-retrieval-at-use-time vs. summarization-into-skills/docs are not mutually exclusive alternatives to weight updates. Storing raw logs and summarizing at use time IS itself a form of context/memory engineering, not evidence that summarization in general is useless — it says *pre-compressing eagerly* may lose information vs *retrieving raw and compressing lazily*. That's a claim about when/how to summarize, not whether to.

3. Misstates the prior claim: continual-learning work saying "current methods plateau" was about weight-update-based continual learning specifically, not a blanket claim that no method (including retrieval/context approaches) can improve. Citing that as if it predicted this result backwards is a misreading — the two literatures are about different mechanisms (parametric vs non-parametric memory).

4. "Doubled agent scores" is likely benchmark- and setup-specific (context window size, task type, retrieval quality) — not a general multiplier that transfers to all agents/domains.

5. Ignores tradeoffs: raw logs at use time cost more tokens/latency/compute per query than a compact skill/doc; for large histories this doesn't scale as well as summarization, and retrieval quality matters. "Totally obsolete" ignores these real engineering tradeoffs.

6. Conflates "no RL needed" (a claim about training method) with "summarization is useless" (a claim about information architecture) — these are separate axes; the study's finding on one doesn't logically bear on the other.

In short: the post takes a specific, likely narrow empirical result and inflates it into a sweeping, mutually-exclusive claim ("summaries obsolete") via a false dichotomy and unsupported generalization, while also misrepresenting what the prior continual-learning literature actually claimed.
