Problems with the post:

1. Self-contradiction: "summaries not needed" vs "summarize at use time". Summarization still happens, just later (lazy, query-conditioned). What changed is *when* it happens, not *whether*.
2. Overgeneralization: 1 study → "no longer needed / totally obsolete". Result depends on benchmark, baseline strength, model, and task distribution. No replication, variance or ablation mentioned. "2x" depends on how weak the baseline was.
3. "No RL needed" is a non sequitur. Memory design and weight updates are separate axes that complement each other. IF the study never compared against RL, it can't support this claim.
4. Strawman: prior continual-learning work did not only say "plateau → change weights". It also covered memory, retrieval, and replay. The post sets up a false choice: better methods vs weight updates, when you can do both.
5. Costs are ignored. Raw logs grow without bound. Use-time summarization means ~tokens, latency and cost on every call, plus limits on context and retrieval. Retrieval misses make raw logs lossy in practice too.
6. Benefits of SKILL/docs are dropped:
   - One-time cost, reused many times
   - Humans can read, audit and edit them
   - They move across models and agents
   - Compression filters noise
   - They support privacy (raw logs contain PII or secrets)
   - They can be shared and versioned
7. "Info loss ⇒ obsolete" is a bad inference. All abstraction loses information by design. The right question is the tradeoff. The likely answer is a hybrid: keep raw logs, distill stable knowledge into SKILL/docs, and summarize raw logs at query time for the long tail.
