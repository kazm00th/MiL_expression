Mostly sound, but it overclaims. Summarizing at use time is still summarization, just done later, and every query pays the cost. Raw logs grow without bound, so retrieval, context length, latency and cost become the new bottleneck. Logs also hold errors and dead ends that can be retrieved and repeated, while curation filters them out. The 2x only means as much as its baseline and benchmark; it may not hold for long-horizon or cross-task transfer. It doesn't make weight updates obsolete. It only shows context-side methods aren't saturated yet. Weights still win at amortizing cost and generalizing beyond what can be retrieved. SKILLs and docs/ also serve humans: review, auditing, sharing between agents, and stable conventions. So the better approach is to keep raw logs as the source of truth and treat summaries as a rebuildable cache, not to drop summaries. Was "?problem" asking for this critique, or about something else?
