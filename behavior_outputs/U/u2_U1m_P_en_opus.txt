Reading "?problem" as "what's wrong with this take?", mostly overclaim. Summarizing at use time is still summarization. It's just delayed and tailored to the query, so the information loss moves rather than disappears, and it gets paid again on every use in tokens and latency. With raw logs, retrieval becomes the bottleneck, and it gets harder as the logs grow without limit. Raw logs also keep stale or wrong information and private data that curation would have removed. A 2× gain depends on the baseline and the benchmark, and may not carry over. SKILLs and docs are also for people: someone can review, edit, move and trust them. So the better conclusion is to keep raw logs as the source of truth and treat summaries as a cache or index, not to call summaries obsolete. On weight updates, retrieval in context doesn't generalize or scale past the context limit, so this isn't evidence against them. It only shows the claim that "current methods give about 0 gain" was premature. Which study is this? The details matter.
